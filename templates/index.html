Home energy decisions are becoming more complicated. Solar panels, batteries, heat pumps, EV chargers, financing programs, utility rules, and warranties all matter. Asking every homeowner to figure this out alone is inefficient.

Solar as the first cohort opportunity

Solar is a practical, visible subject for a first group purchase: it is easy to picture, easy to talk about with neighbours, and often more attainable than adding batteries right away. That is why this pilot leads with solar.

Solar may not be the first technical improvement every house needs. Roof condition, shading, insulation and electrical capacity — and future heat-pump or EV charging demand — can matter more for some homes. For whole-home planning, talk to Better Homes Ottawa or an independent energy advisor before committing.

Battery backup instead of a gas generator

Many households are considering generators because of storms and power outages. Solar panels alone do not normally power a home during an outage. Backup power requires compatible battery, inverter, isolation and electrical equipment.

Batteries are not the right answer for every home, and they can be expensive. But if several households are already considering generators, a coordinated solar-plus-battery option may be worth comparing clearly and honestly.

How neighbours build momentum

People act faster when they see nearby homes moving first. This process is designed to make the choice visible, social, and easy to join.

  1. Register interest. Share your name, email, and general location. No purchase commitment.
  2. Watch the count grow. The cohort tracker shows how many nearby homes have registered interest so far.
  3. Invite local contractors. Once enough nearby homes register, we ask contractors to quote for the cluster of homes.
  4. Compare options openly. Residents see equipment, warranties, timelines, and pricing side by side.
  5. Choose individually. Every household decides on its own. Group momentum makes the process easier, not mandatory.
  6. Share what worked. Yard signs, window decals, and short neighbour stories help the next block move faster.

If your home already performs well, you still matter. Climate Pioneers make efficient choices visible, answer questions, and set the local norm.

Common questions from residents

Does this cost anything to join?

No. Registering your interest is free and non-binding. There is no commission on any contractor deal, and no fee to be part of the cohort.

Am I obligated to buy anything?

No. Every household decides independently whether and when to sign a contract. Group momentum makes the process easier to navigate — it does not commit you to a purchase.

What if my home already has solar or an efficient setup?

You can still register as a Neighbourhood Climate Pioneer. Visible local action helps the next household feel that upgrading is normal here.

How does a bigger group help me get a better price?

Grouped site visits, sales and scheduling may reduce contractor costs when they can quote for {% for t in cohort.thresholds %}{{ t }}{% if not loop.last %}, {% endif %}{% endfor %} homes at once instead of chasing single leads. The cohort will ask whether that allows better pricing or warranty terms, and any resident benefit must be shown in the submitted proposals.
