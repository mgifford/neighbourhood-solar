# Meeting agendas

Three meetings come up in a typical pilot cycle: the info session (once
you hit your first sign-up threshold), the vendor-selection meeting
(once bids are in), and the post-installation debrief (once the first
homes are done). Agendas below are starting points — adapt timing to
how many households show up.

## Info session agenda

**Purpose:** convert "I registered interest" into "I understand the
process and I'm still in." Roughly 45-60 minutes, in person or video
call.

1. **Welcome and why we're here** (5 min) — who organized this, why,
   and that it's resident-led with no one taking a commission.
2. **How the cohort model works** (10 min) — group size thresholds,
   what "strength in numbers" actually gets the group (pricing,
   warranty terms, scheduling), and that nobody is obligated to buy
   anything at any stage.
3. **What happens next, step by step** (10 min):
   - The group finalizes its target size and closes registration for
     this round (or keeps it open — decide as a group).
   - Organizers draft and send an RFP / vendor questions to a shortlist
     of local installers (see [vendor-questions.md](vendor-questions.md)).
   - Vendors present or submit bids.
   - The group compares bids using shared criteria (see
     [evaluation-criteria.md](evaluation-criteria.md)) and picks a
     preferred vendor, or a short ranked list.
   - Each household still gets and signs its own individual contract —
     the group negotiates collectively, but no one signs on anyone
     else's behalf.
4. **Incentive programs overview** (10 min) — walk through the
   `programs` list from the site config relevant to your jurisdiction.
   Be clear this is general information, not personalized financial
   advice.
5. **Questions** (10-15 min).
6. **Next steps and who to contact** (2 min) — reiterate the sign-up
   link for anyone who came without registering yet, and how the group
   will communicate going forward (email list, WhatsApp group, etc.).

**After the meeting:** send a follow-up email summarizing what was
covered and the next milestone/date, even to people who couldn't
attend.

## Vendor-selection meeting agenda

**Purpose:** compare bids as a group and reach a decision (or short
list) transparently. 60-90 minutes once bids are in hand.

1. **Recap the process so far** (5 min) — how many vendors were
   contacted, how many responded, timeline since the info session.
2. **Present each bid using the same format** (10-15 min per vendor) —
   go through the shared [evaluation criteria](evaluation-criteria.md)
   for every vendor in the same order, so comparisons are apples-to-apples
   rather than whoever presented last being freshest in people's minds.
3. **Open floor for questions on each bid** (as needed).
4. **Group discussion** (15-20 min) — strengths, concerns, anything
   that came up in reference checks.
5. **Decision** (10 min) — how you decide is up to the group (straw
   poll, informal consensus, organizers make the final call after
   hearing input). Decide and announce the method *before* discussion
   starts, not after, so the process feels fair regardless of outcome.
6. **Next steps** (5 min) — how individual households now proceed to
   get their own quote/contract from the selected vendor(s), and the
   expected timeline.

**Keep a written record.** Whoever didn't attend should be able to read
a short summary of why the group chose what it chose — this is also
what future households (and future cities forking this project) will
learn from.

## Post-installation debrief agenda

**Purpose:** capture what worked and what didn't while it's fresh,
both for the households who just went through it and for whoever runs
the next cohort. 30-45 minutes, a few weeks after the first
installations are complete.

1. **How did it go, household by household** (15-20 min) — timeline
   vs. what was promised, any surprises (cost, scheduling, quality of
   work), whether the group pricing/warranty terms actually held.
2. **What would you tell the next cohort** (10 min) — concrete
   lessons: what to ask vendors that wasn't asked, what to double-check
   in a contract, anything about the incentive programs that turned out
   to be harder or easier than expected.
3. **Update the playbook** (10 min) — assign someone to actually fold
   these lessons back into this document (or the vendor
   questions/evaluation criteria docs) so the next round starts smarter
   than this one did.
4. **Is there a second cohort** (5 min) — appetite and rough headcount
   for another round, and whether the same organizers are running it.
