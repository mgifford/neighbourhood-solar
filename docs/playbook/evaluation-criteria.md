# Evaluation criteria

A shared way to compare vendor bids so the group isn't just picking the
lowest number. Use this alongside the answers gathered from
[vendor-questions.md](vendor-questions.md).

## Suggested scoring approach

A simple weighted scorecard works better than a gut-feel discussion,
especially with a group of strangers who don't have pre-existing trust
in each other's judgment. One approach:

1. Agree on categories and weights *before* seeing any bids (see table
   below for a starting point — adjust weights to what the group
   actually cares about).
2. Score each vendor 1-5 in each category, independently, then compare
   notes as a group. Independent-then-compare avoids the loudest voice
   in the room anchoring everyone else's score.
3. Multiply each score by its weight and sum for a total per vendor.
4. Use the scorecard to structure discussion, not to replace it — a
   close numeric score between two vendors is a prompt to talk it
   through, not an excuse to skip the conversation.

| Category | Suggested weight | What to look at |
|---|---|---|
| Price (per kW, total cost) | 25% | Not just the headline number — compare against what's actually included |
| Equipment quality and warranty | 20% | Panel/inverter brand reputation, manufacturer warranty length |
| Workmanship warranty | 15% | Years covered, what's included, how claims are handled |
| Licensing, insurance, certification | 15% | Pass/fail more than a scale — a vendor missing required licensing shouldn't be scored, just excluded |
| References and track record | 15% | Quality of references, years in business, any group-install experience |
| Timeline and responsiveness | 10% | Lead time, communication during the RFP process itself (how they handled *this* process is a preview of how they'll handle the job) |

Adjust weights for your group's priorities — a group more concerned
about long-term reliability than upfront cost might weight equipment
and warranty higher than price.

## Comparing group discount structures

Vendors may structure group pricing differently (flat percentage off,
tiered by group size, bulk equipment discount passed through). Convert
every offer to the same units — total estimated cost per household at
your actual group size — before comparing, rather than comparing
headline discount percentages directly.

## Beyond the scorecard

- **Check references before finalizing**, not just after — a reference
  check that surfaces a problem should be able to change the outcome,
  not just confirm a decision already made.
- **Read the actual contract language**, not just the sales pitch,
  before the group commits. Warranty terms, cancellation terms, and
  payment milestones matter more than what's said verbally.
- **It's okay to shortlist rather than pick one.** If the group is
  split, or the group is large enough, presenting two vendors and
  letting each household choose between them (still at group pricing)
  can work better than forcing a single winner.
- **Document the decision and why**, even briefly — see the
  vendor-selection meeting agenda in
  [meeting-agendas.md](meeting-agendas.md#vendor-selection-meeting-agenda).
  This record is what the next cohort — in your neighbourhood or
  someone else's city — learns from.

## After the decision

Each household proceeds to get and sign its own individual contract
with the selected vendor(s) at the negotiated group terms. The group
negotiates collectively; no one signs a contract on anyone else's
behalf, and no household is obligated to proceed even after the group
selects a vendor.
