# Vendor questions

Questions to send to contractors bidding for group business, and things
to watch for in their answers. This project is vendor-neutral — the
point of asking every vendor the same questions is to make comparison
fair, not to steer toward a preferred outcome.

## Before contacting vendors

- Agree as a group (or with whoever's organizing) on a shortlist size —
  3-5 vendors is usually enough to compare meaningfully without
  overwhelming the group.
- Send the same written question set to every vendor, at the same time,
  with the same deadline. This is both fairer and gives you directly
  comparable answers.
- Be upfront about the group size and that this is a competitive,
  transparent process — vendors should know they're one of several being
  asked the same questions.

## Licensing, certification, and insurance

- Are you licensed to do electrical/solar installation work in this
  jurisdiction? (Ask for the license/certification number, and verify
  it independently rather than taking the answer at face value.)
- Are your installers certified by the relevant national body (in
  Canada: check for CSA-listed equipment and installers certified for
  the specific incentive program you're applying under, if any)?
- What insurance do you carry, and can you provide proof of liability
  coverage?
- How many years has your company been doing residential solar/battery
  installation specifically (not just electrical work generally)?

## Pricing and what's included

- What is your per-kW price, and what does the quote include
  (equipment, labour, permits, inspection, interconnection paperwork)?
- What's explicitly *not* included, and billed separately (roof
  repair, electrical panel upgrade, tree removal, scaffolding)?
- What group discount can you offer at [N] households, and does the
  discount scale further at larger group sizes?
- Do you require a deposit, and what are the payment milestones?
- Will you help households apply for relevant incentive programs, or is
  that the homeowner's responsibility?

## Equipment and workmanship

- What panel and inverter brands do you install, and why did you choose
  them?
- What's the manufacturer's warranty on panels, inverters, and battery
  storage (if applicable)?
- What's your own workmanship warranty, separate from the manufacturer's
  equipment warranty? (This is often the more meaningful number — a
  25-year panel warranty is not the same as a 25-year installation
  warranty.)
- What happens if a component fails during the workmanship warranty
  period — who do we call, and what's your typical response time?

## Timeline and process

- What's your current lead time from signed contract to installation?
- How many crews do you have, and could you realistically install for
  [N] households within [timeframe]?
- Who handles permits and utility interconnection paperwork, and what's
  the typical timeline for that step specifically (this is often the
  slowest part, not the physical install)?
- What happens if there's a delay — do you communicate proactively, or
  do we need to chase updates?

## References and track record

- Can you provide three references from installations completed in the
  last 12 months, ideally in a similar neighbourhood or house type?
- Are you willing to have the group visit a completed installation, or
  speak directly with a past customer?
- Have you done a group/cohort installation before? What went well or
  poorly?

## Red flags to watch for

- Reluctance to provide license numbers, insurance proof, or references
  in writing.
- Pressure to sign quickly, or discounts that expire in a suspiciously
  short window ("today only").
- A workmanship warranty significantly shorter than the equipment
  warranty, with no clear explanation.
- Vague answers about who handles permits/interconnection, or an
  unusually long estimated timeline for that step.
- Unwillingness to put the group discount and its conditions in writing.
